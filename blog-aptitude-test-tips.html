How to Clear Aptitude Tests for IT Companies: Tips, Tricks & Practice Questions

📅 February 2026⏱ 9 min read 👤 InterviewEdge Team🏷️ Aptitude

The aptitude test is the first major filter in campus placement and off-campus drives for Indian IT companies. Many technically strong candidates fail this round because they underestimate it or prepare too late. This guide gives you a complete strategy to clear aptitude tests at TCS, Wipro, Cognizant, HCL, and similar companies — with specific techniques, practice approaches, and the most important topic areas to focus on.

Why the Aptitude Round Is Critical

In large campus hiring drives, aptitude tests are used to quickly screen thousands of candidates. At TCS's NQT, for example, only the top 20–30% of scorers typically proceed to the technical round. Many of the eliminated candidates are technically capable — they simply did not prepare adequately for the aptitude test format and time pressure.

The good news: aptitude tests are highly predictable. Companies use the same types of questions year after year. With 6–8 weeks of focused preparation, even candidates who struggle with math can learn to solve these problems quickly and accurately.

Understanding the Format: What Each Company Tests

TCS NQT Aptitude Section

TCS's aptitude round (part of the NQT) covers numerical ability, verbal ability, and reasoning ability. Numerical questions focus on the standard topics listed below. Verbal covers reading comprehension, fill-in-the-blanks, sentence correction, and vocabulary. Reasoning includes series completion, blood relations, directions, seating arrangements, and syllogisms. The section is strictly timed — accuracy and speed are equally important.

Wipro Aptitude Test

Wipro's online aptitude section typically includes quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, and verbal English. It also includes a written communication (essay) component which is unique to Wipro. The essay is evaluated on grammar, coherence, vocabulary, and relevance.

Cognizant (GenC) Aptitude

Cognizant's aptitude test is known to be moderately difficult with an emphasis on data interpretation and logical reasoning. Time management is crucial as questions can be lengthy.

Infosys and HCL

Both companies include numerical reasoning and verbal sections, with Infosys's questions typically being slightly easier than TCS's NQT. HCL focuses heavily on basic aptitude and logical reasoning with moderate difficulty.

Quantitative Aptitude: Topics and Strategies

High-Priority Topics (appear in almost every test)

  • Percentages: The foundation of profit/loss, discount, and data interpretation problems. Master the shortcut: percentage increase = (change/original) × 100.
  • Time and Work: Learn the LCM method for solving complex problems. If A can do a job in 'x' days and B in 'y' days, together they complete 1/x + 1/y of the work per day.
  • Time, Speed, Distance: Relative speed concepts (same direction: subtract, opposite: add) are frequently tested. Practice average speed problems — a common trap question.
  • Profit and Loss: Know SP, CP, marked price, and discount formulas cold. Practice chain discount problems.
  • Number Series: Identify the pattern (arithmetic, geometric, difference-of-differences, square/cube-based). Practice recognising patterns in under 20 seconds.

Medium-Priority Topics

  • Permutations and Combinations: Know nCr and nPr formulas. Practice "arrangements with restrictions" — they are common in TCS.
  • Probability: Basic probability (favourable outcomes / total outcomes). Conditional probability at a basic level.
  • Simple and Compound Interest: Know the formulas and be able to calculate CI using (1 + r/n)^(nt) quickly.
  • Data Interpretation: Bar graphs, pie charts, tables, line graphs. Practice reading data quickly and performing mental calculations.

Speed-Building Techniques

Raw speed in aptitude tests comes from three things: knowing formulas cold (no time spent trying to recall), practising shortcuts (Vedic maths, approximation techniques), and regular timed practice. Here are specific speed techniques:

  • Multiply by 5: Divide by 2 and add a zero. 136 × 5 = 136/2 × 10 = 680. Faster than long multiplication.
  • Percentage of a number: For 18% of 250, calculate 10% (25) + 8% (20) = 45. Breaking percentages into 10%, 5%, 1% components is faster than the formula.
  • Approximation: For data interpretation, round numbers to nearest 5 or 10 and approximate. This is faster and usually accurate enough to identify the correct option.

Verbal Ability: How to Improve

Verbal ability is the section most candidates neglect — and the section where significant marks can be gained quickly with the right approach.

Reading Comprehension

Read the questions first before reading the passage. This primes your brain to look for relevant information. For inference-based questions, the answer must be fully supported by the passage — do not bring in outside knowledge. Practice with passages from The Hindu, BBC News, or previous-year placement papers.

Error Spotting and Sentence Correction

Most errors tested are: subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, pronoun agreement, preposition usage, and articles (a/an/the). Read quality English content daily — errors become obvious with exposure.

Fill in the Blanks and Vocabulary

Read the sentence in full before looking at options. Eliminate options that are grammatically wrong first, then choose from remaining options based on meaning and tone. Build vocabulary with 5–10 new words per day using a word-a-day app or flashcard system.

💡 Verbal Tip: Spending 20 minutes reading The Hindu's editorial section daily for 6 weeks will noticeably improve your reading speed, vocabulary, and error-spotting ability — more than any dedicated verbal aptitude book.

Logical Reasoning: Pattern Recognition

Logical reasoning tests your ability to think systematically. The key is recognising question types quickly and applying the right method.

Blood Relations

Draw a family tree diagram the moment you see a blood relations question. Never try to solve these in your head. A quick diagram takes 20 seconds to draw and saves 2 minutes of confused reasoning.

Seating Arrangements

Similarly, always draw the arrangement (circular or linear) as you read the clues. Place definite information first, then work through conditional clues. These questions take 3–5 minutes each but are highly scorable once you have a systematic approach.

Syllogisms

Use Venn diagrams to solve syllogisms. All A are B → B's circle completely contains A's circle. Some A are B → the circles partially overlap. No A are B → circles do not touch. This visual method eliminates ambiguity.

Exam Day Strategy

Your strategy on the actual test day is as important as your preparation:

  • Attempt easy questions first: Scan through the section and mark all questions you can solve in under 30 seconds. Answer those first, then return to harder ones.
  • Manage time per question: In a 30-question, 30-minute section, you have 60 seconds per question on average. If a question is taking more than 90 seconds, skip it and return later.
  • Negative marking awareness: For tests with negative marking (typically -0.25 for wrong answers), only guess if you can eliminate at least 2 options confidently. Random guessing hurts your score.
  • Check the calculator policy: Some online aptitude tests allow an on-screen calculator, some do not. Know this in advance and practise accordingly.

6-Week Study Schedule for Aptitude

  • Week 1: Percentages, Profit & Loss, Simple Interest — theory + 50 practice problems
  • Week 2: Time & Work, Time-Speed-Distance, Compound Interest — theory + 50 problems
  • Week 3: Number Series, P&C, Probability — theory + 30 problems + first mock test
  • Week 4: Logical Reasoning — all question types with timed practice
  • Week 5: Verbal ability — RC, error spotting, fill-in-the-blanks daily
  • Week 6: Full company-specific mock tests (PrepInsta, IndiaBix company tests) + review weak areas

Best Free Resources

  • IndiaBix.com: Excellent for topic-wise practice with detailed solutions
  • PrepInsta.com: Company-specific previous year papers and mock tests
  • TCS iON Practice: Official TCS practice platform (register with your email)
  • R.S. Aggarwal — Quantitative Aptitude: The most comprehensive book for QA preparation
  • Arun Sharma — How to Prepare for Quantitative Aptitude: Strong shortcut techniques

💡 Final Advice: Quality beats quantity in aptitude preparation. 50 carefully reviewed problems per day — where you understand every solution fully — is more valuable than 200 problems rushed through. After each mistake, understand why your approach was wrong before moving on.

Aptitude tests are a skill, not a measure of intelligence. With consistent, focused preparation over 6–8 weeks, any motivated candidate can clear aptitude rounds at even the most competitive IT companies.
